# The Sophistication Trap

A pitfall card from Analytics Pitfalls & Principles, drawn from *The Auction Block* by Brian Cariveau (https://www.analyticspitfalls.com/book).
https://www.analyticspitfalls.com/cards/37

## How to use this card

Use it when what someone is working on, or something they heard at work, looks like the problem below: a plan, a roadmap, a request, a dashboard, a metric, an AI project.

1. Name the card by its title and say in a sentence why it fits. Say the work looks like this pitfall; never tell someone they have it.
2. Give the principle in the card's own words. Don't rewrite it.
3. Take the steps one at a time against their actual situation: what is already done, what isn't, and which to do first. If you need a fact to do that, ask one question, not five.
4. If the card doesn't fit, say so plainly. Don't stretch it.
5. Finish with the card's link: https://www.analyticspitfalls.com/cards/37

## The problem

Making everything complex because it feels smart. Simple feels too easy, like you're not doing "real strategy." So you create 147-slide strategy decks that nobody reads. Complex architectural roadmaps that nobody understands. Sophisticated frameworks with 47 components that nobody can remember. Leaders love complexity because it looks impressive in board meetings. Consultants love complexity because it justifies their fees. But complexity? Complexity is what kills execution. Every time.

## The principle

SIMPLICITY IS THE ULTIMATE SOPHISTICATION: Simple is what scales. Simple is what teams can execute. Simple is what actually transforms organizations. 

The 60-second test: If you can't explain your strategy/goal/approach in 60 seconds to someone new, it's not simple enough. Kill the complexity. Ask "what's the simplest version?" then do THAT version.

Real examples that win: One-page visual everyone references beats fancy 50-slide deck on a wall. Clear "we're building toward THIS" beats complex architectural roadmap. Three numbers everyone knows beats sophisticated dashboard with 40 charts.

Focus on 1-3 things, not 10 priorities. Make it visual—one simple picture beats 10 pages of text. Remove, don't add—transformation comes from removing complexity, not adding process. When you need the WHOLE team to move, clarity matters more than completeness.

Always choose simple. Every. Single. Time.

## The steps

1. Scrap the 147-Slide Deck: Rebuild your strategy on ONE page. Three sections: Where we are. Where we're going. How we get there. That's it.
2. Apply the 60-Second Test: Practice explaining your strategy to someone new in 60 seconds. If you can't, it's too complex. Simplify until you can.
3. Kill Jargon Everywhere: Rename metrics from jargon to plain English. Replace complex frameworks with simple visuals. If your grandmother wouldn't understand it, rewrite it.

## In practice

A chief data officer inherited a disaster: a 147-slide "Data Strategy 2025" deck that nobody had actually read. Leadership team got 30 minutes into the presentation before eyes glazed over. Strategy adoption: 12%. Nobody knew where they were going.

The CDO scrapped it. Started over with one rule: fit it on one page.

The new strategy visual showed three things: 

WHERE WE ARE NOW: 6 data sources, 23 tools, 8 teams working separately, duplicating effort everywhere.

WHERE WE'RE GOING: 1 platform, 3 tools, 1 team, zero duplication.

HOW WE GET THERE: 3 quarterly milestones with clear deliverables.

That's it. One page. Everyone could see it, understand it, remember it.

Strategy adoption went from 12% to 94% in three months. Why? Because everyone—from executives to junior analysts—could explain the strategy in 30 seconds. Board meetings became productive instead of confusing. Quarterly reviews focused on progress, not explaining what the strategy was.

The 147-slide deck gathered digital dust. The one-page visual went on every wall and every team homepage.

Simple won.
