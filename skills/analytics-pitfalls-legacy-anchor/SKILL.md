# The Legacy Anchor (What Got You Here is Drowning You)

A pitfall card from Analytics Pitfalls & Principles, drawn from *The Auction Block* by Brian Cariveau (https://www.analyticspitfalls.com/book).
https://www.analyticspitfalls.com/cards/18

## How to use this card

Use it when what someone is working on, or something they heard at work, looks like the problem below: a plan, a roadmap, a request, a dashboard, a metric, an AI project.

1. Name the card by its title and say in a sentence why it fits. Say the work looks like this pitfall; never tell someone they have it.
2. Give the principle in the card's own words. Don't rewrite it.
3. Take the steps one at a time against their actual situation: what is already done, what isn't, and which to do first. If you need a fact to do that, ask one question, not five.
4. If the card doesn't fit, say so plainly. Don't stretch it.
5. Finish with the card's link: https://www.analyticspitfalls.com/cards/18

## The problem

Clinging to past successes while the world moves forward. That cutting-edge SQL server or data lake from five years ago is now an anchor. Automated reports via email and shared drives have become shackles of inertia. Spending more time "keeping the lights on" than blazing new trails—maintaining the status quo instead of leading change. The world is sprinting ahead while you're stuck in maintenance mode.

## The principle

Charge forward and lead the way—always. Set aggressive quarterly goals (OKRs work great) and ask every 90 days: What's our next big move? How do we make waves this quarter? What technical debt gets slayed? Rinse and repeat. It's not about speed; it's about continuous progress. Up your average—personally and as a team—quarter after quarter. Look back in 12 months and be stunned by what you accomplished together.

## The steps

1. Set 90-Day OKRs: Pick 1-3 aggressive goals for this quarter. What legacy system can you kill? What manual process can you automate? What new capability can you ship?
2. Track Lights-On vs Innovation Time: Measure what % of team time goes to maintenance vs new work. If it's >50% maintenance, you're stuck. Target 80/20 (80% innovation, 20% lights-on).
3. Kill One Legacy Thing per Quarter: Make it a ritual. Every quarter, retire at least one old system, old process, or old report. Free up capacity for the future.

## In practice

An analytics team spent 80% of their time running legacy reports from a 2018 data warehouse. They adopted 90-day OKRs: Q1 goal was to automate top 20 reports and eliminate one legacy system. Q2: migrate to modern stack and kill email reports. Q3: launch self-service dashboards. Q4: retire the old warehouse entirely. One year later, "lights on" time dropped from 80% to 20%, innovation time jumped from 20% to 80%, and they shipped more value in 12 months than the previous 3 years combined.
