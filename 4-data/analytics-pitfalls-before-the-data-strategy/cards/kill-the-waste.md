# Kill the Waste (The Lean Analytics Playbook)

A principle card from Analytics Pitfalls & Principles, drawn from *The Auction Block* by Brian Cariveau (https://www.analyticspitfalls.com/book).
https://www.analyticspitfalls.com/cards/26

## How to use this card

Use it when what someone is working on, or something they heard at work, looks like the problem below: a plan, a roadmap, a request, a dashboard, a metric, an AI project.

1. If you don't know their situation yet (what they're working on, and where: their kind of company and team), ask one short question first, unless the conversation already says. Then fit everything below to it, not to a generic company.
2. Name the card by its title and say in a sentence why it fits. Say the work could use this principle.
3. Give the principle in the card's own words. Don't rewrite it.
4. Take the steps one at a time against their actual situation: what is already done, what isn't, and which to do first. If you need a fact to do that, ask one question, not five.
5. Then push past the technical fix, if the work needs people to work differently (most does). The steps are the if-then part. Ask what has to change in how people work, what they have to learn or believe, or what they give up, for it to stick. That's the adaptive work (Ronald Heifetz's term), and it's usually the real work.
6. If the card doesn't fit, say so plainly. Don't stretch it.
7. Finish with the card's link: https://www.analyticspitfalls.com/cards/26

## The problem

Walk through your analytics "factory" and look for waste. Dashboards nobody opens—waste. Reports that take 3 days to generate when users only need it monthly—waste. Analysts spending 60% of their time on manual data pulls instead of analysis—waste. Pipelines breaking weekly because they're brittle—waste. Meetings where no decisions get made—waste. Teams rebuilding the same analysis because they don't know someone already did it—waste. Most analytics teams operate with 50-70% waste and just accept it as "the cost of doing business." But waste isn't neutral—it's expensive. It burns budget, crushes morale, and prevents you from doing high-value work. Every hour spent on waste is an hour not spent on innovation.

## The principle

Adopt a lean manufacturing mindset: identify waste, eliminate it ruthlessly, free up capacity for value creation. Map your processes—what takes time but doesn't create value? Kill it. Automate manual work. Retire unused assets. Streamline workflows. Say no to low-value requests. Track your team's time—what percentage goes to maintenance vs innovation? Target 80/20 (80% innovation, 20% maintenance). The magic happens when you free your team from the grind of keeping the lights on and unleash them to build the future. Less waste, more impact. That's the path to powerhouse status.

## The steps

1. Track Time for 2 Weeks: Have your team log what they spend time on. Categorize: Innovation vs Maintenance vs Waste. Calculate the %. If waste >20%, attack it.
2. Automate the Top 3 Manual Tasks: What's eating your team's time? Automate it. Build self-service tools. Kill manual reports. Free up 10+ hours/week minimum.
3. Set a Waste Reduction Target: Aim for 80/20 split - 80% innovation, 20% maintenance. Track it monthly. Celebrate when waste goes down.

## In practice

A SaaS analytics team tracked time: 75% maintenance (fixing broken dashboards, manual reporting, ad hoc requests), 25% innovation (new features, analysis, insights). Unsustainable. They launched "Kill the Waste" sprints: (1) Automated 23 manual reports—freed 30 hours/week. (2) Retired 15 unused dashboards—stopped spending time on maintenance. (3) Built self-service tools for common ad hoc requests—deflected 60% of asks. (4) Fixed 5 chronically broken pipelines—stopped firefighting. Six months later: 30% maintenance, 70% innovation. Team morale skyrocketed. Shipped 3x more new features. Leadership noticed: "You guys seem way more strategic now." Same team, zero waste. That's the transformation.
