# Rearview vs Windshield (Stop Driving While Staring Backwards)

A principle card from Analytics Pitfalls & Principles, drawn from *The Auction Block* by Brian Cariveau (https://www.analyticspitfalls.com/book).
https://www.analyticspitfalls.com/cards/63

## How to use this card

Use it when what someone is working on, or something they heard at work, looks like the problem below: a plan, a roadmap, a request, a dashboard, a metric, an AI project.

1. If you don't know their situation yet (what they're working on, and where: their kind of company and team), ask one short question first, unless the conversation already says. Then fit everything below to it, not to a generic company.
2. Name the card by its title and say in a sentence why it fits. Say the work could use this principle.
3. Give the principle in the card's own words. Don't rewrite it.
4. Take the steps one at a time against their actual situation: what is already done, what isn't, and which to do first. If you need a fact to do that, ask one question, not five.
5. Then push past the technical fix, if the work needs people to work differently (most does). The steps are the if-then part. Ask what has to change in how people work, what they have to learn or believe, or what they give up, for it to stick. That's the adaptive work (Ronald Heifetz's term), and it's usually the real work.
6. If the card doesn't fit, say so plainly. Don't stretch it.
7. Finish with the card's link: https://www.analyticspitfalls.com/cards/63

## The problem

Your exec dashboard shows: last month's revenue, last quarter's pipeline, yesterday's customer count. All lagging indicators—rearview mirror metrics. Leadership meeting: "Revenue was down last month. What happened?" You spend 2 hours explaining the past. Nobody talks about what's coming. It's like driving a car while staring in the rearview mirror—you see where you've been, not where you're going. By the time lagging metrics show problems, it's too late to fix them. You're always reacting, never preventing. The past is useful for learning, but obsessing over it means you'll crash into the future.

## The principle

Balance rearview (lagging) with windshield (leading) metrics. Lagging metrics tell you what happened (revenue, conversions, churn). Leading metrics tell you what's about to happen (pipeline velocity, trial signups, usage trends). The magic happens when 70% of your metrics are forward-looking. Think driving: you glance at the rearview occasionally, but mostly watch the windshield. Same with metrics. Dashboard should show: short-term performance (rearview, 30%) and long-term leading indicators (windshield, 70%). Shift meetings from "what happened?" to "what's coming and what do we do?"

## The steps

1. **Audit Your Metric Balance**

List all metrics on your main dashboards.

Classify each:
- **Lagging (rearview)**: What happened (revenue, conversions, costs)
- **Leading (windshield)**: What's coming (pipeline, trials, trends, early warnings)

Calculate: % lagging vs leading.

Most teams: 80%+ lagging. That's a problem.

Goal: Flip to 30% lagging, 70% leading.
2. **Build Windshield Metrics**

For each lagging metric, ask: "What predicts this 2-4 weeks early?"

Examples:
- Revenue (lagging) → Pipeline velocity (leading)
- Churn (lagging) → Usage decline, support tickets (leading)
- Costs (lagging) → Approval rate drops, error rates (leading)

Add these leading indicators to your dashboard.

Bonus: Add forecasts. Show where metrics are heading, not just where they've been.
3. **Restructure Leadership Meetings**

Old format: 60 min explaining what happened (all rearview)

New format:
- 10 min: Rearview (what happened, brief)
- 50 min: Windshield (what's coming, what to do)

Prepare for meetings by analyzing:
- What's trending up/down?
- What early warnings fired?
- What decisions do we need to make now to affect future outcomes?

Goal: Shift from reactive (explaining past) to proactive (shaping future).

Bonus: Track "decisions made proactively vs reactively." Aim for 70%+ proactive.

## In practice

A healthcare analytics team had classic lagging-heavy dashboard: patient volume (last month), satisfaction scores (last quarter), costs (last month). Every leadership meeting was backward-looking: explaining what happened, no discussion of what's coming. New leader asked: "What's our windshield dashboard?" They created leading indicator view: Patient appointment booking trends (3-week forward indicator), Member contact rate spikes (early warning), Claim denial patterns forming (leading to cost increases). Added 90-day forecast for every metric. Leadership meetings transformed: First 10 minutes on rearview (what happened), 50 minutes on windshield (what's coming, what to do). Result: caught patient volume drop 3 weeks early (proactive marketing campaign), spotted denial pattern forming (fixed before cost spike), prevented satisfaction issue (early intervention on complaint trends). Moved from reactive to proactive. The rearview didn't disappear—it just stopped being the only view.
